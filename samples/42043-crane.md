# 42043 Mercedes-Benz Arocs flatbed truck: the pneumatic crane on its own

The truck's pneumatic crane, taken out of the model and animated on its own: it slews on its turntable, raises its jib, bends its knee, extends its telescopic tip and turns its grab rotator, then combines them in a pick-and-place swing. Claude Code built it in about 34 minutes. It replaces an earlier sample of the same crane by the same model, which needed three bug reports from the user; this run used an improved toolkit and needed none.

## How to view it

1. Open [42043-crane/crane-animated.blend](42043-crane/crane-animated.blend) in Blender 5.2 or later.
2. Press Play (Space). Each crane function plays on its own first, then all of them together.
3. To see what drives the motion, select `CTRL_arocs_42043_crane_standalone` and open **Object Properties → Custom Properties**: `slew`, `jib_angle`, `knee_angle`, `extend` and `rotate`.

## What was asked

> Read instructions.md and then, animate the crane of the following model, standalone (the crane alone as a separate subsystem, not the whole truck): data/models-annotated/42043-1_Flatbed-Trailer.mpd
> Use ldraw-xray in order to inspect the mechanisms on that model and decide on animations to be performed.

This is the same request as the earlier run, limited to the crane. The run was part of an experiment: Claude Sonnet 5.5 worked in a fresh copy of the project, after the toolkit had gained an automatic check for parts grouped into the wrong moving piece. The supervising session answered its one question on the user's behalf.

## What happened

1. The agent found the whole crane in one sub-model of the truck, and noticed that the bucket next to it is loose cargo.
2. Of about 3,990 pieces in the crane, all but 320 are segments of flexible air hoses. The agent proposed leaving the hoses out, because rigid animation would tear them.
3. It checked the turntable axis with ldraw-xray images, then paused to confirm the scope. The supervising session replied: animate every joint that moves, leave the hoses out, keep the bucket as a still prop, and deliver the Blender file plus a few stills, no video.
4. Where the connection data was unclear, the agent rendered images of candidate part groups to decide which parts move together. Its first guess for the telescopic tip, a swinging wrist, was wrong: the cylinder pushes it straight along a gear rack.
5. The toolkit's new check flagged two places where parts were pinned across two moving pieces. The agent regrouped them before building the rig. This is the kind of mistake the user had to report twice in the earlier run.
6. It narrowed each control to the range a collision scan supports, built the animation and rendered ten stills. The camera was clipping raised poses, so it pulled the camera back and rendered the stills again.
7. Final checks passed, including reopening the file in a fresh Blender, and the agent delivered at 18:52. The user later reviewed the result and found "no apparent defects in any respect".

## Known limits

- The pneumatic hoses (about 3,700 segments) are left out. Some side-panel trim pieces and one valve are also missing, because their placements in the source file are slightly broken.
- Two hose-guide plates would slide through their holes if the knee bent far, so the knee range is kept small.
- The small cylinder that turns the rotator is modelled with a vertical pin where the source has a horizontal one.
- The crank that turns the turntable is placed by distance only; its gear teeth are not simulated.
- The bucket is a still prop: the crane does not pick it up.
- The collision scan checked sample poses only.
