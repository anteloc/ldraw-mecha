# 3625 Fabuland plane: take-off, flight and landing

A small red LEGO Fabuland plane with its seagull pilot takes off, flies and lands. The plane stays on the spot: it only tilts, banks and rises, while the propeller spins, the wheels turn and the pilot looks around and steers. Claude Code built the rig and the animation from the LDraw model in about 12 minutes.

## How to view it

1. Open [3625/animated.blend](3625/animated.blend) in Blender 5.2 or later.
2. Press Play (Space).
3. To see what drives the motion, select `CTRL_model` and open **Object Properties → Custom Properties**:
    - `prop`, `wheel`, `head`, `arm_l` and `arm_r` turn the propeller, the wheels, the pilot's head and arms;
    - `climb`, `bank` and `lift` tilt and raise the whole plane.

## What was asked

## What happened

1. The agent read the project instructions and proposed a plan.
2. The user rejected it, saying a rendered video would be "a waste of time and effort". The agent dropped all renders and delivered only the Blender file.
3. The propeller and the wheels were stored as single combined pieces, so they could not turn on their own. The agent made a copy of the model with them split into separate pieces, and used that copy from then on.
4. It checked the axis of every joint (propeller, wheels, head, both arms) with images made by the ldraw-xray tool.
5. It built the animation: engine start, take-off roll, climb, cruise with gentle banking, descent, touchdown with one small bounce, roll-out and engine stop. The only movement of the plane is upwards (at most about 5 cm in real LEGO scale), so it never leaves its spot.
6. A collision scan found the pilot's head passing through the pilot's right arm at the most extreme poses. The agent made the head turns and arm swings smaller, and the new scan found no such overlaps.
7. It checked that the saved file reopens correctly and still matches the plan, then delivered at about 16:45.
8. At 17:26 the user asked how long the work took and how useful ldraw-xray had been. The agent could not give a time, because it only saw file dates. It rated ldraw-xray "moderately useful": it confirmed the joint axes, but the collision and the combined pieces were found by other means.

## Known limits

- The collision scan looked at every 6th frame, so a brief overlap between those frames could be missed. It also does not notice a part sitting completely inside another.
- The wheels spin as scripted, not because they roll on a ground.
- The pilot's legs and hips stay still inside the cockpit.
- Only the propeller has an automatic check that it stays on its pin. The other joints were checked visually.
- The source file's description of the pilot ("An empty submodel…") is wrong. It was left unchanged.
