# 42000 Technic Grand Prix Racer: steering, differential and V8 engine rig

A LEGO Technic Formula 1 car rigged so that turning the steering wheel steers the front wheels through the real gear train, and turning the rear wheels drives the differential and the eight-piston engine. It was the hardest of three test models while Codex built this project's animation toolkit, so the result is a rig you move by hand, not a recorded animation.

## How to view it

1. Open [42000/rigged.blend](42000/rigged.blend) in Blender 5.2 or later.
2. Select `CTRL_technic42000` and open **Object Properties → Custom Properties**.
3. Drag the sliders: `steering` turns the steering wheel and, through it, the front wheels (up to about ±58°). `spin_fl`, `spin_fr`, `spin_rl` and `spin_rr` turn the four wheels; spinning the rear wheels at different speeds shows the differential and turns the engine.

## What was asked

The user asked for a reusable Python toolkit that lets agents analyse an LDraw model, find its mechanisms and rig them in Blender realistically, validated on progressively harder models. A hand-made rig of this car, written earlier by Claude Opus 5.5, was given as the quality target.

## What happened

1. The agent opened the existing hand-made rig of this car in Blender and studied it: 22 working drivers and controls for steering, speed and the four wheels.
2. It turned that rig's approach into the toolkit's reusable plan format and rebuilt the car through it, reusing the reference rig's measurements and part selections.
3. It checked the plan with pure maths first: both steering tie rods and all eight engine connecting rods keep their length at every tested position.
4. In Blender, all 1,378 parts were matched to the plan. Spinning the rear wheels at different speeds gave the expected differential motion.
5. Across 75 sampled positions the new rig matched the reference rig almost exactly (within about two millionths of a unit).
6. The session sat idle for almost three hours until the user said "continue". Final checks passed, and the agent delivered at 03:52, together with the other test models.
7. The agent noted that the user's own `42000-1-animated.blend` had changed during the session, and left that change in place.

## Known limits

- There is no recorded animation: the file is a rig whose controls you move by hand.
- The car stands still. Driving it along a path is left to the hand-made reference rig.
- No collision scan was run for this car. The steering limit comes from the reference rig.
- Motion is ideal and rigid: no backlash, friction or forces.
