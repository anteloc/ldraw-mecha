# 42110 Land Rover Defender: every inner mechanism at work, bodywork hidden

The LEGO Technic Land Rover Defender with its body panels hidden, so you can watch the inner mechanisms move: the four-wheel drivetrain with three differentials, two gearboxes, the six-cylinder engine, the steering and the winch. The animation drives off, shifts through the gears, steers from lock to lock, reverses and winds the winch. Claude Code built it in a little over an hour.

## How to view it

1. Open [42110/animated.blend](42110/animated.blend) in Blender 5.2 or later.
2. Press Play (Space). The body panels are hidden in Blender only; nothing was removed from the model.
3. To see what drives the motion, select `CTRL_defender_42110` and open **Object Properties → Custom Properties**:
    - `drive`, `steering` and `winch` move the wheels, the steering and the winch drum;
    - `gear_select`, `range_select` and `direction_select` shift the gearboxes;
    - `diff_*` split the wheel speeds in turns;
    - `phase_*` are helper values that keep the shafts smooth when gears change. Leave them as keyed.

## What happened

1. The agent surveyed the toolkit and asked three questions. The user chose to hide the body panels but keep the frame visible, to keep the suspension still, and to receive a Blender file plus stills, with no video.
2. Several gears and the gearbox's selector were stored inside the model in a way the toolkit could not see. The agent fixed the toolkit so it recognises them, and fixed two problems with matching parts in the 3D model.
3. It worked out every mechanism: 91 moving pieces, 37 gear pairs, and every combination of gear, range and direction (16 in all). All of them turn at exactly the right ratios.
4. It hid 1,512 body parts in Blender, and built the animation: drive off, shift 1st to 4th, switch to high range while moving, steer lock to lock, stop, shift down, reverse with steering, then the winch.
5. Blender's normal accuracy check failed by a hair on a model this large, a rounding effect of the program's number precision. The agent loosened the tolerance from 0.002 to 0.01 units and recorded why.
6. A collision scan left some overlaps in place, rather than hiding them by narrowing the ranges. Most are expected: gear clutches engaging, gear-change forks, and the winch's ratchet pawl, which does not move.
7. The agent delivered at 01:13. That evening the user asked why the diagrams in `diagrams/` show almost no connections. The agent admitted they were a by-product it never used: the real model of how parts connect lived only in its build script. It judged that a checkable map of connections, stored in the plan, would help future work.

## Known limits

- The suspension stays at its resting height, as the user chose. The doors, bonnet and other hinged body parts are hidden and not rigged.
- Some overlaps remain, most of them expected. The steering links also graze the lower suspension arms on and off over part of the steering range.
- Two shapes are educated guesses: the cam that selects the 4-speed gears and the cams that lift the engine's pistons.
- No friction, backlash, gravity or flex.
- The ropes and hoses stay still: the winch rope does not unwind.
