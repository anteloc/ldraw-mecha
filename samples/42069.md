# 42069 Extreme Adventure: running tracks, steering and V8, in place

The LEGO Technic Extreme Adventure, a four-track off-roader, drives without leaving its spot: all 100 track links run around their triangular pods, the pods tilt over a wave of bumps, the front pods steer, the rear axle rocks with its shock absorbers, and the V8 engine's pistons pump. The bodywork stays visible. Claude Code built the rig and the 25-second animation from the LDraw model in about 70 minutes.

## How to view it

1. Open [42069/ai-animated.blend](42069/ai-animated.blend) in Blender 5.2 or later.
2. Press Play (Space). The camera `MainCamera` shows the whole vehicle from the front; switch to `TrackCamera` for a low side view of the tracks.
3. To see what drives the motion, select `CTRL_extreme_adventure_42069` and open **Object Properties → Custom Properties**:
    - `drive` runs the tracks, the drivetrain and the engine (positive is forward);
    - `diff_front` and `diff_rear` let the left and right tracks run at different speeds in turns;
    - `steering` turns the knob on the roof and, through it, the front pods (positive steers left);
    - `pod_fl`, `pod_fr`, `pod_rl` and `pod_rr` tilt each track pod;
    - `rear_roll` rocks the rear axle side to side.

[42069/ai-rigged.blend](42069/ai-rigged.blend) has the same rig without the animation, for posing by hand.

## What was asked

> Animate the following model: data/models-annotated/42069-1.mpd
>
> Make it stay in place, to avoid chasing it in Blender.
> At the end of the process, provide an estimate for the animation process invested time.

## What happened

1. The agent surveyed the model (3,257 parts) and asked four questions. The user chose the drivetrain and engine, the steering and the pod and suspension movement (leaving out the winch and doors), circulating track links, the whole model visible, and a Blender file plus stills with no video.
2. The model was too large for the toolkit's automatic search for touching parts, so the agent ran it on the 1,471 mechanical parts only, and worked out every shaft and gear pair from that.
3. Each track link has to follow its loop, which no existing kind of joint could do. The agent added one to the toolkit: a looping table of positions (the `path` kind), with tests and documentation.
4. To let the springs in the shock absorbers stretch, it worked from a copy of the model in which only the four spring shapes are stored differently ([42069/source/42069-1-springs.mpd](42069/source/42069-1-springs.mpd)). The original file is untouched.
5. It measured the drivetrain, steering, pods and rear suspension, checked each joint's axis with 25 look boards made by the ldraw-xray tool, and built the rig. Blender's accuracy check needed a looser tolerance (0.08 instead of 0.002 units, about 0.03 mm), because a still shovel accessory is stored slightly skewed in the source; moving parts stay within 0.0015 units.
6. Collision scans showed the pods hitting the frame and a steering joint clipping its column at the larger angles. The agent narrowed the pod tilt and the steering lock (about 12°) until the scans came back clean.
7. It animated pull-away, steering left and right, a bump wave rocking the pods and the rear axle, reversing and settling; checked the file after reopening; rendered eight stills; and handed over at 13:00 with the time estimate the user asked for (about 70 minutes, phase by phase in [42069/REPORT.md](42069/REPORT.md)).

## Known limits

- The tracks follow their loops by calculation, not by contact. Gaps of up to about 0.3 mm open between links, and up to 0.5 mm at the source model's own uneven closing link. Where the tracks wrap the sprockets and idlers they touch them, as in the source model.
- The front suspension stays still: moving it would bend the steering column. The winch, ratchet, linear actuator, doors and rope stay still too, by choice, and the steering wheel is not connected to the steering in the model.
- Steering lock is about 12° and pod tilt is limited to stay clear of the frame, so the range is smaller than the real model allows.
- When the rear axle rocks, the shock rods, arms and the rear propshaft joint cut slightly into neighbouring parts (estimated at under 0.4 mm, not measured).
- Springs, damping, gravity and friction are not simulated. The collision scans only sampled some poses; the motion is not claimed to be collision-free.
- The new `path` joint kind and its tests were changes to the toolkit that were not yet committed when the sample was added.
